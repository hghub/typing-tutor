## The Core Freelancer Mistake

Freelancers often look at one strong month and assume that number is their salary. It is not. Business revenue must first cover:

- business costs
- tax reserve
- operating reserve
- emergency runway
- only then: owner pay

## A Worked Cash-Allocation Example

For illustrative monthly cash receipts of PKR 450,000, business payments of PKR 70,000 and an independently chosen annual tax cash budget of PKR 120,000, monthly tax funding is PKR 10,000. A selected 15% operating reserve allocates PKR 67,500. If household costs are PKR 180,000, the selected six-month household target is PKR 1,080,000. Starting with PKR 450,000 leaves PKR 630,000 to build; over twelve months, that allocates PKR 52,500 monthly.

The remainder for owner withdrawals is PKR 250,000. This is an arithmetic scenario, not a recommended tax budget or reserve percentage. If receipts fall 20% while those allocations stay fixed, the remainder falls to PKR 160,000, below the entered household costs. A plan with negative remaining cash reports a shortfall instead of hiding it behind a zero-withdrawal result.

## Why Cash Timing Matters in Pakistan

Independent workers in Pakistan often face delayed payments, FX swings, platform instability, and family cost pressure at the same time. Without reserve discipline, one weak quarter can force panic withdrawals or bad debt decisions.

## A Practical Reserve Structure

A sensible first structure is:

- **Tax reserve:** keep a separate monthly tax bucket
- **Operating reserve:** keep a percentage of monthly inflows for slow months and client delays
- **Emergency reserve:** build a personal runway target measured in months of home expenses

## What Changes the Reserve Percentage?

- income volatility
- number of active clients
- how much of your income is in foreign currency
- whether you have dependents
- how stable your fixed monthly expenses are

## Owner Pay Should Be the Output, Not the Starting Point

The wrong sequence is: receive payment → spend personally → worry about tax later. The better sequence is: receive payment → reserve → pay business costs → decide owner pay from what remains.
