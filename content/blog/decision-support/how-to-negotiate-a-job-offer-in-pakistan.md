## Negotiate the Weak Point, Not Everything at Once

Many people know an offer feels close but still negotiate vaguely. That makes the conversation weaker than it needs to be. A better approach is to identify **which variable actually changes the decision**.

## The Usual Negotiation Levers

- fixed monthly salary
- joining bonus or relocation support
- remote days or commute flexibility
- medical cover
- fuel or transport support
- learning budget or professional development

## When to Ask for Salary vs Flexibility

If tax and city-cost pressure are the main issue, a stronger fixed salary or recurring allowance matters most. If the offer is being weakened by commute burden and energy loss, remote flexibility may improve the decision faster than a small salary adjustment.

## What Makes Negotiation More Credible

Do not negotiate with only emotion. Show that you evaluated the package on after-tax value, benefits, commute, and practical quality of life. That makes the request sound reasoned instead of impulsive.

## A Good Framing

You are not asking for “more because why not”. You are trying to close the specific gap that currently makes the role hard to justify.

## Separate Cash, Benefits And One-Time Support

Compare recurring after-tax cash against a household budget you have checked yourself. Keep insurance cover, provident fund and one-time relocation support in separate columns. They may have value, but cannot all be spent each month. The evaluator's package-minus-example-budget result is not a savings forecast, and its weighted scores are not probabilities. Avoid counting an allowance twice if it is already included in gross salary.
