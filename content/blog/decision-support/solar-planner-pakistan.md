## Compare a solar quote with the electricity you can actually use

A useful solar decision starts with consumption and a written scope, rather than a promised zero bill. A large installation can generate many units while exporting or curtailing much of them. Backup is a separate service with its own equipment, capacity and replacement costs.

## Collect evidence before comparing quotes

Read monthly kWh from several bills, ideally including seasonal extremes. Ask the installer for a site-specific monthly yield estimate, shading assumptions, panel and inverter specifications, installation and protection scope, applicable taxes, warranty exclusions and written connection costs. Record when each quote was issued and when it expires. A quoted PV capacity is not proof that the roof, sanctioned load, transformer or connection can accommodate it.

Use an **avoidable energy charge**, not simply the whole bill divided by units. Fixed charges and some other amounts may remain after solar. The planner does not calculate slab changes, time-of-use bills, tax treatment or export settlement eligibility. Ask the licensee to explain the applicable tariff and agreement.

## Follow the arithmetic

Monthly generation = quoted kW × daily peak sun hours × 30 × output-after-losses percentage. Monthly self-use is generation × entered self-use percentage, capped by entered monthly consumption. This cap prevents claiming more use than the household consumes, but does not establish that loads coincide with sunlight. An interval load profile is needed for that.

Self-use value = self-used kWh × avoidable import charge. In new net-billing mode only, surplus × entered export rate is shown separately as estimated credit. In no-export-value mode the surplus has no financial value and is labelled unvalued/curtailed. That mode does not establish electrical curtailment capability or approval exemption.

Total upfront = installed PV quote + additional connection cost + additional battery cost. Enter each cost once. Annual benefit is twelve months of energy value less maintenance and any replacement in the selected year. The annual table shows generation degradation through declining benefit and the selected replacement expense. Sustained recovery is measured at year end, without interpolating an invented precise payback date.

## A reproducible synthetic example

With 5 kW, 5 peak sun hours and 80% output after losses, the model produces 600 kWh per 30-day month. With 600 kWh consumption and 60% self-use, it uses 360 kWh and leaves 240 kWh surplus. At an **illustrative** PKR 40 avoidable charge and no export value, monthly avoided energy cost is PKR 14,400. An PKR 800,000 PV quote plus PKR 50,000 additional connection cost totals PKR 850,000. With PKR 10,000 annual maintenance, no battery or replacement cost and 0.5% annual generation decline, first-year net benefit is PKR 162,800 and sustained recovery occurs at the end of year 6. These figures are a test case, not a claim about current prices, tariffs or your house.

Change daytime usage, yield, quote, maintenance and replacement assumptions separately. If a replacement reverses an early recovery, the displayed recovery moves later or is not reached. The 30-year nominal total is not net present value, a product-life guarantee or an investment recommendation.

## Keep batteries and backup honest

A battery cost enters upfront spending without automatically increasing self-use. The calculator does not simulate charge/discharge efficiency, power limits, depth of discharge, usable capacity or backup duration. Do not raise self-use just because a battery is quoted; have the installer demonstrate the energy flows and essential-load design. A standard grid-connected arrangement must not be assumed to power the house during an outage.

## Compare evidence, then decide

Save the text scenario with quote references kept privately outside public content. Ask the installer to reconcile generation, self-use, approval costs and exclusions against it. Compare at least a smaller self-use-focused system and the proposed larger installation. Do not accept a fixed payback promise based on rising tariffs or full retail value for every generated unit.

Use the [Solar Planner](/tools/solar-planner) with measured monthly kWh, a written installed quote, an avoidable import-energy charge and a documented export rate if applicable. ## Which policy applies? Reviewed 24 November 2026

The final Prosumer Regulations took effect on **9 February 2026**. For new net-billing arrangements, imports use the applicable tariff and exports use the national average energy purchase price. Do not substitute a remembered PKR 11 rate or treat export credit as retail-rate savings. The calculator supplies no current numeric export rate. Verify the rate applicable to your connection with the licensee. Regulation 14 permits excess billed credit to carry to the next billing cycle or be paid quarterly; it is not a promise of cash in the same month.

The **2 April 2026 amendment**, deemed effective from 9 February, preserves the former rate and mechanism for valid agreements executed before commencement until their expiry, subject to its exception for a material modification changing maximum electrical output. Existing-agreement billing is **not modelled** by either calculator mode. Ask the licensee to confirm the agreement, expiry and proposed modification before comparing a replacement or expansion.

The **28 April fee notification**, also effective from 9 February, sets the Authority concurrence fee at nil for facilities up to 25 kW and PKR 1,000/kW above that threshold. The **6 August amendment** removes Authority concurrence for facilities of 25 kW or below, with approval by the concerned licensee. This does not remove approval, metering or interconnection requirements and costs. Regulations 5 and 13 allow bidirectional measurement using one meter or two. Regulation 12 allows direct procurement if licensee meters are unavailable, subject to licensee testing. Obtain the current written equipment and connection requirements rather than assuming a separate meter or a blanket private-purchase ban.
