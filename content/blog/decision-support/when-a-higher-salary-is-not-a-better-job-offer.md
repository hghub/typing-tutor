## The Headline Number Can Mislead You

A higher salary offer feels like obvious progress. But in practice, many people discover that the better-looking package was only better on paper.

The reason is simple: the monthly number is only one part of the decision. What matters is the **full practical value** of the offer after tax, commute, relocation, flexibility, and employer-paid support are included.

## Five Ways a Higher Salary Can Still Be Worse

1. **Tax takes more than expected:** The jump in gross pay does not fully convert into spendable cash.
2. **Commute becomes a hidden deduction:** More travel cost and more time drain can quietly destroy the gain.
3. **The new city is more expensive:** A move to a costlier city can reduce your true monthly breathing room.
4. **Benefits are weaker:** Poor medical cover, weak provident fund support, or no learning budget can reduce the total package materially.
5. **Flexibility collapses:** Fewer remote days or a harsher schedule can make a higher salary feel worse in daily life.

## What to Compare Instead

A better salary-offer comparison should include:

- after-tax take-home
- annual bonus realism
- commute and travel burden
- medical, fuel, and other employer-paid support
- provident fund contribution
- remote flexibility
- growth support and learning budget
- city-level cost pressure

## The Right Decision Question

Do not ask only, “Is the salary higher?” Ask this instead:

**“Does this offer create a better life and stronger financial position after all the hidden adjustments are made?”**

## Separate Cash, Benefits And One-Time Support

Compare recurring after-tax cash against a household budget you have checked yourself. Keep insurance cover, provident fund and one-time relocation support in separate columns. They may have value, but cannot all be spent each month. The evaluator's package-minus-example-budget result is not a savings forecast, and its weighted scores are not probabilities. Avoid counting an allowance twice if it is already included in gross salary.
